# MiniProject3 — Validation Reflection (cwolver1)

Reviewing Sergio Macias Ayala's ten MiniProject2 projects showed why an inactivity gap should not automatically be interpreted as the death of a project. Some projects have a pause followed by a substantial return to work. For example, `giaf_hpipm` had a five-month gap and later resumed bug fixes and feature development. In contrast, `bc_retina` had a much longer gap and only a single day of CI and configuration cleanup in January 2025. I learned that the number, type, and continuity of post-gap commits matter more than the mere presence of a new commit.

Several signals preceded longer gaps. Research projects such as `caseywdunn_siphonophore_phylogeny_2017` became quiet after publication and data release, which may indicate that the original research objective was completed rather than that the project failed. In other cases, commit histories shifted toward maintenance, automated dependency updates, or sporadic documentation edits. Recovery can also mean different things: `prjemian_spec2nexus` resumed packaging and release work after a long pause, but much of the subsequent activity remained automated. This is a partial return, not necessarily a return to continuous feature development.

The main interpretation challenge was separating repository activity from meaningful development. We agreed to classify `malbecc_lio` as **Inactive under the default-branch interpretation** of the specified 2025 window, while acknowledging that commits on other branches could support a different interpretation. For `antonyuryev_elsevierapi`, we selected **Unverifiable** because the GitHub repository was no longer accessible; the available WoC history does not by itself verify the GitHub status required by the rubric. We also reconciled differences in `WhyRecovered` wording where both descriptions referred to no sustained recovery or no meaningful gap, and standardized the order of the two recent-theme categories for `prjemian_spec2nexus`.

Overall, the validation process helped distinguish a **gap**, a **brief maintenance event**, and a **sustained recovery**. Commit messages, author continuity, time-series trends, and the specified activity window were all important. The strongest resolutions came from defining the classification rule explicitly and then checking whether the evidence actually supported it, rather than relying on a single recent timestamp or an ambiguous commit message.
